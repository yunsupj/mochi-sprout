# Phase 0, Day 4 — EMA and variable observation delay

**Learning gate:** PASSED by the project owner before this implementation. **Engineering status:** measured simulated evidence, awaiting human review.

## Architecture and concepts

The existing [PID](../src/mochi_sprout/control/pid.py) remains unchanged. The simulator-independent [observation module](../src/mochi_sprout/simulation/observation.py) now adds scalar `EMA` and `DeliveryPipeline` beside Day 3's fixed-delay classes. `EMA` initializes from the first accepted value and resets between episodes. Alpha 1 transmits each accepted value; smaller alpha gives the past more weight. With a 10 ms control period, alpha 0.1 has a longer effective memory than 0.8. It can suppress high-frequency noise while delaying a real angle change. The PID proportional term sees the filtered angle, and derivative-on-measurement differentiates the filtered values at each controller tick. The filter itself updates only on accepted measurements, not repeated stale decisions.

The [MuJoCo adapter](../src/mochi_sprout/simulation/controlled_pendulum.py) remains responsible for true state and torque integration. It records current raw sensor, latest accepted raw sample, filtered controller input, generation timestamp, delivery timestamp, decision timestamp, and actual age. The [scenario runner](../src/mochi_sprout/experiments/day04.py) orchestrates tests; the [evaluator](../src/mochi_sprout/evaluation/processing_metrics.py) reuses Day 3 true-state tracking metrics and adds accepted, dropped, stale, filtered-error, and scheduled-delay statistics. These observation utilities can serve another scalar angle controller, but a full interchangeable limb interface has not been built.

Every sensor sample is generated at a controller tick, assigned a seeded delivery delay, and placed in a timestamped priority queue. At a decision tick, only samples whose arrival time has passed are processed. Equal arrival times use generation order. A sample older than the newest accepted measurement is dropped; an out-of-order arrival never replaces a newer accepted state. The initial zero-angle prior is used until a delayed real measurement arrives and is marked as such. No real-time waiting or future state is used. Fixed 50 ms and uniform discrete 10/30/50/70/90 ms jitter have the same nominal mean delay; the accepted age differs because delayed old packets are dropped.

## Findings and interpretation

**HYPOTHESIS:** EMA should reduce command fluctuation under sensor noise, but its lag might harm tracking, especially with variable delivery timing.

**MEASURED:** The [evidence table](../experiments/day04/README.md) and [per-seed summary](../experiments/day04/summary.json) show the Day 3 clean baseline exactly: 0.313° final-window MAE and 3.630 s settle. With 0.4° noise and no filter, mean adjacent-command RMS was 1.715 N·m. Alpha 0.8 reduced it to 1.242, alpha 0.2 to 0.245, and alpha 0.1 to 0.150 N·m. Yet alpha 0.1 raised post-step RMSE to 4.450° and current-time filtered error RMS to 2.050°, compared with alpha 0.2's 4.126° and 0.695°. Both metrics include lag, not just noise. Five-seed final-window MAE alone would incorrectly favor alpha 0.1.

**MEASURED:** Jitter's mean scheduled delay was 50.048 ms across the five seeds, near fixed 50 ms. Its selected accepted observations had mean age 31.922 ms because out-of-order old samples were discarded. A mean of 550.8 messages per 1,000 decisions was dropped. Noise-free jitter settled in all five seeds but had mean post-step RMSE 4.463°, versus 4.177° for fixed 50 ms. The noise-plus-jitter unfiltered case also settled in all five seeds. Adding alpha 0.2 to that combined case led to four trajectories exceeding ±360° and one finite but unsettled run. No settling time was imputed as zero.

**INTERPRETATION:** Variable arrival order and EMA memory can create a damaging feedback lag under these gains. The combined failure is visible in the [physical trajectory plot](../experiments/day04/combined_tracking.png), not merely in a computed metric. This is a specific result for the tested delay distribution and discard policy. It does not prove filtering is generally unsafe or that fixed delay is universally better than jitter.

**NOT VERIFIED:** A state predictor was considered but not implemented after the core combined case failed. A bounded future baseline could estimate velocity from two accepted, distinct timestamps and extrapolate only up to a capped horizon, falling back when history is insufficient. Such a prediction would be an estimate, never measured ground truth. No Kalman filter, hardware sensor, real network, contact task, different initial state, alternative target, gain retuning, or sim-to-real claim is included. The one 10 s target step, five seeds, and one jitter distribution limit generality. EMA has no timestamp-aware adaptation beyond accepted-sample updates; changing the controller period changes its effective response.

For future interchangeable humanoid limbs, sample timestamps, arrival order, and source freshness must be explicit at the observation boundary. This one-hinge pendulum only validates a small interface and simulated behavior, not a limb design.

## Focused PR #3 instability investigation

**MEASURED:** The [diagnostic evidence](../experiments/day04/README.md#follow-up-engineering-diagnosis) adds only jitter/no-noise/EMA 0.2, noise/fixed-50-ms/EMA 0.2, and noise/jitter/EMA 0.8. Jitter plus EMA 0.2 failed all five seeds even with zero noise. Noise plus fixed 50 ms and EMA 0.2 stayed finite but did not settle. Noise plus jitter and EMA 0.8 settled all five seeds. This isolates the strongest failure to the combination of irregular accepted timing and substantial EMA memory with the fixed PID gains; noise is not required.

**MEASURED:** Independent replay of 1,000 generated samples per run matched all queue counts: 995 delivered by the 9.99 s decision, five pending, and 542–570 outdated drops across the five jitter seeds. The mean 550.8 drops is 55.36% of delivered samples. Accepted measurement timestamp gaps averaged about 22–23 ms and reached 70–80 ms. For paired comparisons, the sampled delays and Gaussian noise residuals matched exactly across filter settings. The MuJoCo hinge has no joint limit and true `qpos` crosses ±360° in four original combined runs; the angle is unwrapped.

**MEASURED:** True-angle divergence from the paired unfiltered run first exceeded 5° at 0.686 s (seed 11) and 0.684 s (seed 23). The [onset trace](../experiments/day04/diagnostic_onsets.csv) records timestamp, true angle and velocity, raw and filtered angles, age, P/I/D terms, unclipped and clipped torque, and saturation. At 0.690 s for seed 11, true angle was 28.04° but the filtered angle was 11.89° from a 30 ms old sample; P still commanded +1.896 N·m. A stale decision produced zero derivative term; accepted updates nearby produced negative derivative pulses. This pattern is consistent with feedback phase lag and irregular derivative response. The integral contribution near onset was about +0.156 N·m, much smaller than P and derivative pulses.

**INTERPRETATION — confirmed within this model:** The queue is causal and its high drop fraction follows from the configured jitter distribution and monotonic acceptance rule. No implementation defect was found. EMA 0.2 updates only when accepted packets arrive, so its effective timing is irregular. The PID's derivative uses a fixed 10 ms decision interval; it therefore sees zero change on stale ticks and concentrated changes on acceptance ticks. Combined with filtered-angle lag, this drives prolonged actuator saturation and genuine closed-loop oscillation. This mechanism is supported by the paired isolation scenarios and controller traces, but it is not a general stability proof.

**NOT VERIFIED:** Other delay distributions, timestamp-aware filter coefficients, retuned gains, prediction, hardware, or full robot joints. The failing configuration must not be treated as validated for hardware use. The original nine scenario metrics and figures remain unchanged.
